# Round 2 review — TAL (Talent Acquisition & International Recruitment Expert)

## 1. Overall read

The strongest ideas share one move: they change what is asked for instead of policing how it was written. Scarce intent (signals), claims anchored in the person's own words, and proof produced live in front of a human are signals AI cannot mass-produce. They are also need-first: one company card, written three weeks out, drives the pool preview, the conversation questions and the closure reasons. And they keep one rule: machines arrange, people decide.

From the recruiter's side of the booth, the binding constraint is company minutes: 5–50 companies against 200–1,000 talents. Winners save recruiter time or raise the value of each minute (Sparks, Challenge Tables, one-tap rubrics). Anything that adds company homework must pay back before the ask (pool counter, €5,000 timing alert). On the talent side, anything longer than uploading a CV must give something back: an answer.

Over-engineered for 20 hours: on-device matching with signed catalogs, verifiable credentials, CP-SAT plus load tests, stable matching with forced floors, twin-lab dashboards as features, and Shared Hire.

The jury will love "fluent Finnish: 6 → English: 41", "point, don't write", a dozen signals instead of 140 messages, and a desk that says "don't sign yet".

## 4. My top 10

1. UX-04 — Kills the AI-written cover letter at the source: pairing a story with a problem line is quick for humans, pointless for bots.
2. TECH-06 — Brings the best-evidenced selection format (structured, ρ≈.42 vs .19) into the booth with no AI; Espoo could run it at the next event.
3. PM-01 — Fixes P2 upstream and changes the evaluation, not just the ad; every later step reuses its card and verification questions.
4. PM-08 — Ends ghosting and puts permits and public supports at peak hiring intent, the City's uncopyable value (P1, P9).
5. UX-07 — Best answer to scarce company minutes (18 people an hour per host) while capability is shown live, write-first, with a remote seat.
6. PM-04 — The must-answer rule turns scarce signals into guaranteed replies for talent; merge with Sparks as the signal rulebook.
7. STR-01 — The 30-second rebuttal to "no suitable candidates": requirement elasticity makes inclusion the company's self-interest.
8. UX-01 — Voice-first, strongest-language evidence intake that real jobseekers, including second-language and low-literacy ones, can actually finish.
9. UX-03 — Language is the top barrier for internationals in Finland; a two-sided situational grid makes requirements discussable, justifiable and fair.
10. UX-06 — Zero-app, cheap and memorable: an opening line for introverts, relevance at a glance for recruiters, and our best stage prop.

## 5. Red flags

- RAI-02 — An unconditional "one meeting for every talent" breaks at ~1,000 talents and ~180 slots, and forced floor meetings recreate P4 for companies. Deliver the floor through Circles, Challenge Tables and Connectors, never forced 1:1s.
- STR-09 — Multi-employer part-time contracts raise labour-law, collective-agreement and benefit-adjustment issues, and roles under 80% lose the €5,000 support. Drop from the MVP.
- TECH-07 — A City-issued "witnessed" credential brings issuer liability and uneven witness standards, and it rewards people who already got meetings (P10). Post-pilot only, with legal wording.
- STR-05 — 180-day individual outcome tracking plus employment-register cross-checks, by a City that is also the benefit authority: purpose limitation and a DPIA. Demo aggregates only.
- RAI-05, STR-01, PM-02, TECH-10 — Counts can over-promise (registered ≠ attending; self-declared ≠ verified) and leak through differencing queries. Label them "evidence, not availability"; use bands, k ≥ 10 and fixed query shapes.
- UX-05 — "AI-drafted" chips can become a disclosure-based AI detector that punishes honest users and non-native writers (P10, C5). Treat AI-assisted text as neutral; promote only the witnessed and introduced rungs.
- UX-04, UX-09, PM-04, TECH-05 — AI "fit" suggestions about people bring Annex III scope and GDPR profiling. Keep them deterministic, unranked and explained, and never let them order company inboxes (C1).
- STR-03 — Eligibility rests on age and mother tongue (protected grounds). Keep it out of discovery and ordering; an advisor checks only after mutual interest (Burtless's stigma finding).
- UX-01, PM-03, RAI-03, TECH-02 — Voice stories capture health, family or religion details (GDPR Art. 9). Auto-flag them for removal, delete audio, disclose the AI (Art. 50) and never infer emotion.
- PM-04, STR-04, TECH-08 — At a five-employer event three signals are not scarce. Scale signals to the number of employers, and keep walk-in time so signals never gate access.

## 6. Recommended winning concept

**ProofDay — the TalentMatch where the CV comes last.**

**Combines (7):** PM-01, STR-01, UX-01, UX-04, UX-07, TECH-06, PM-08. Rules absorbed as settings rather than separate screens: TECH-01's linter, RAI-03's no-quote-no-claim guard, PM-04's must-answer deadline, and TAL-08/TAL-09 inside the next-step desk.

**Demo story:** T-21: Sanna, founder of a synthetic 14-person Espoo software SME, answers a 12-minute voice Need Interview (PM-01). Her wish list becomes a Challenge Card: three 90-day outcomes, three must-haves and how each will be checked. Ticking "fluent Finnish" drops Pool Proof from 41 to 6 (STR-01), so she switches to "English team language, Finnish B1 within a year". T-14: Amira, a backend engineer who moved here with her spouse, tells two work stories by voice (UX-01); every skill on her Story Card quotes her own words, and an inflated claim is refused. She spends one of three Sparks pairing "cut build time from 30 to 6 minutes" with Sanna's line "our CI takes 40 minutes" (UX-04). Sanna must answer 12 evidence pairs, not skim 140 messages. Event day: Amira joins Sanna's 20-minute Challenge Table (UX-07), then a 12-minute Proof Conversation with the same three questions and a one-tap evidence rubric (TECH-06). +72 h: Sanna taps "next step"; the desk warns "don't sign yet: the €5,000 support must be decided first" and books an advisor (PM-08). Everyone else gets an answer and a redirect.

**Cut explicitly:** on-device matching and signed catalogs (TECH-04); verifiable credentials (TECH-07); the forced fairness floor and CP-SAT/load tests (RAI-02, TECH-09), replaced by a greedy mutual-yes scheduler; Shared Hire, Challenge Market and Project Lane (STR-09, STR-08, TAL-10); Demand Radar and the savings ledger (STR-07, STR-05), reduced to one funnel tile; twin-lab dashboards (RAI-08, TECH-11), reduced to one slide and one number; ladder tiers (STR-02), reduced to one T-21 deadline; Bridge Finder (TECH-03).

**Single biggest risk:** company workload. Every step asks scarce company people for something: intake, answering every Spark, hosting a table, closing every loop. If that feels like more work than "send an ad and show up", the 5–50 companies walk (P1). Mitigation: City staff run the intake by phone, every company action is at most two taps, and the payoff (pool counter, fewer but relevant meetings, the €5,000 timing alert) appears before the ask.

## 7. Gaps

1. **A company-minutes budget and a "done-for-you" mode.** Nobody totals what the whole stack asks of a 14-person SME. Cap it (e.g. ≤ 60 minutes outside the event day) and let City staff complete intake and closure by phone.
2. **Hand-off to the company's ATS.** Leads die in spreadsheets. After mutual interest, offer a consented one-click export (card, notes, next step) to e-mail or the company's ATS (e.g. Teamtailor, Recruitee); from then on the company is the controller.
3. **The first 90 days for international hires (P9).** The brief names relocation and onboarding, yet every journey stops at "next step". Reuse the card's 90-day outcomes as the onboarding plan, add the Spouse Program, workplace Finnish and an IHH check-in, and measure 6-month retention.
4. **No-shows.** Free events suffer no-shows, which feeds "free = low quality". Add reconfirmation at T-24 h, human-approved standby fill and a visible attendance rate, with the baseline measured from TalentMatch data.
5. **The job-search duty as a noise source (RAI-07, underweighted).** [Tampere's guidance](https://tamperetyonhakijoille.fi/nain-asioit-meilla/) counts "attend a recruitment event and leave a CV". Count held conversations or answered Sparks instead, firewalled from benefit supervision. Also monitor answer and next-step rates by language group (aggregated, voluntary survey): the hiring bias measured in Finland is human, not algorithmic.
