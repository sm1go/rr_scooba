# Round 2 review — RAI (Responsible AI, Privacy & Legal Lead)

## 1. Overall read

The strongest ideas share one move: they change what is asked for instead of policing how it was written. Scarce intent (five roles proposed signals independently), evidence tied to a person's own words, needs stated as problems weeks ahead and short structured conversations all produce signal an LLM cannot mass-produce, while humans make every decision. They also put AI where regulatory exposure is lowest: on the company side (intake, lint) and as an interviewer the talent can overrule, never as a judge of people.

Over-engineered for a 20-hour build: client-side signed catalogues (TECH-04), verifiable credentials (TECH-07), CP-SAT scheduling and load tests (TECH-09), register-linked savings models (STR-05), multi-employer contracts (STR-09) and full organiser cockpits (PM-09, STR-07).

The jury will love three moments: "fluent Finnish" shrinking the pool to "fewer than 10" and the rewrite growing it to about 40; a meeting request with no essay and no name; and a polished twin and a plain twin getting the same card. No feature trips the hard constraints, so J3 is high almost everywhere and J1/J2 separate the field. The residual risks sit in the talent-side AI intake and in function creep towards benefit supervision.

## 5. Red flags

No feature trips the hard constraints (no AI-text gate, no solely automated decision about a person, no real data in the demo), so none gets J3 ≤ 2. The risks are design-level:

- **STR-05** — Register cross-checks and per-person benefit-savings maths pull event data towards benefit supervision; jobseekers' consent to the City is not freely given (GDPR recital 43). Mitigation: aggregate, talent-confirmed outcomes; register linkage only as pseudonymised statistics under a DPIA.
- **TAL-05** (pilot question) — Counting signals toward the job-search obligation turns them into compliance currency and feeds event data into supervision. Mitigation: only a talent-initiated export of a real conversation (the RAI-07 pattern), never signals.
- **TAL-04** — "Confirmed" by one company's rubric is shown to other employers: third-party data (Act 759/2004 s. 4) and a de facto cross-employer rating. Mitigation: holder-controlled display, positive-only, never sortable.
- **UX-09** — AI pairing of named talent with companies is Annex III(4)(a) high-risk from 2 Dec 2027 (FRIA, logs ≥ 6 months, trained oversight). Mitigation: rule-based overlap suggestions, consent before every intro.
- **TECH-07** — A City-signed credential from a 15-minute witness at a recruiting company: conflict of interest, issuer liability, in-person bias. Mitigation: post-pilot, neutral witnesses, never required.
- **TECH-02, PM-03, UX-01** — AI-assigned quality levels ("specifics", "vague") are AI evaluation of candidates and fluency-biased; accented speech transcribes worse. Mitigation: levels only from facts (artefact attached, witnessed), person-confirmed, parity-tested before each release.
- **STR-01, PM-02, TECH-10, UX-02** — Elasticity toggles are differencing queries, k = 5 is thin at event scale, and showcased stories are free text. Mitigation: k ≥ 10, bands, fixed query shapes, query budgets, per-story consent.
- **UX-03, TECH-05** — "Fits 5 of 6" and per-must-have status tables can become scores that companies "draw strongly on" (CJEU C-634/21, SCHUFA). Mitigation: never sort, filter or rank by them.
- **UX-05** — If recruiters skip "AI-drafted" content, people who need AI help (dyslexia, second language) lose out. Mitigation: chips describe, never filter; translation is labelled apart from drafting.
- **UX-10, PM-06, TECH-10** — 30-day deletion must spare audit logs (AI Act Art. 26(6): ≥ 6 months for high-risk components) and minimal decision records for discrimination claims. Mitigation: one retention schedule agreed with the DPO.
- **TAL-06, UX-07** — Work samples and group tables show adverse impact and favour fluent, confident speakers. Mitigation: practice tasters, extra time, reasonable accommodation, write-first rules.
- **PM-09, STR-06, STR-02** — A public authority picking individuals, awarding public marks or rationing the candidate search must treat everyone equally (Administrative Procedure Act 434/2003 s. 6). Mitigation: published criteria, rationale, rotation, appeal.
- **STR-08** — Written approaches reopen the AI-text channel and invite spec work and IP disputes. Mitigation: Sparks-style pointing plus a live pitch.
- **STR-09** — Multi-employer contracts and competitor coordination: drop from the MVP, keep as a pilot idea.

## 6. Recommended winning concept

**TalentMatch Signal — scarce interest, visible proof, no machine scores.**

**Combines (7):** PM-01, STR-01, UX-01, UX-04, TECH-06, PM-08, TECH-11. Folded-in duplicates: TAL-01 and TECH-01 into PM-01, PM-04 into UX-04, UX-10 into PM-08, RAI-08 into TECH-11.

**Demo story:** T-21: Kuura Logistics' operations manager completes a 12-minute Need Interview (PM-01). The lint questions "native Finnish"; she rewrites it as "Finnish B1 for customer calls from month 6", and Pool Proof (STR-01) jumps from "fewer than 10" to about 40 opted-in people, never a name. Amina, a planner with a three-year caregiving gap, tells three work stories by voice in English (UX-01); every claim on her card quotes her own words, and she deletes one the AI stretched. In signal week she spends one of five Sparks (UX-04), pairing "re-routed 40 deliveries after a depot flood" with Kuura's line "routes collapse when a truck fails": no essay, no photo, no name until Kuura accepts. On the day, the 15-minute Proof Conversation (TECH-06) runs on Kuura's three questions, and Amina sees the same evidence rubric the recruiter taps. Within 72 hours she gets "next step", and the City desk books the recruitment-support check before anyone signs (PM-08). Close: the Twin Test (TECH-11) replays intake for 1,000 twins, plain or ChatGPT-polished, "Amina Warsame" or "Anna Virtanen", and the claims come out identical. Polish buys nothing; a foreign name costs nothing.

**Cut explicitly:** TECH-04 and TECH-07 until after a pilot; TECH-09's CP-SAT and load tests (a greedy schedule and PM-07's static pass suffice); UX-09's AI pairing; STR-05's register linkage; STR-08; STR-09; any match percentage, ranking or "fits N of M" shown to companies; voice beyond Finnish and English in the demo.

**Single biggest risk:** UX-01 is the only AI that processes people's own words. If it extracts weaker claims from accented or second-language stories, we rebuild P10 inside our own product, and it may be Annex III high-risk from December 2027. Mitigation: quote-grounded extraction, person-confirmed lines, and the Twin Test run live on exactly this component against a published threshold.

## 7. Gaps

1. **Controller map and lawful basis.** Nobody defined who controls what: the City (public task, Act 380/2023), companies (their own notes and ratings) and shared booth data (GDPR Art. 26/28). Consent is weak for jobseekers facing a public authority. A one-page data-flow map plus a DPIA/FRIA skeleton is a cheap J3 win.
2. **Companies' own AI.** Every proposal governs our AI; none governs what companies do with event data: AI note-takers, recorded conversations, emotion analysis (Art. 5(1)(f)), cards fed into AI screeners. Needed: participation terms, export limits and a two-minute AI-literacy briefing for reps (Art. 4).
3. **Reasonable accommodation.** Timed tasters, tables and conversations need an accommodation route for disabled participants (Non-Discrimination Act s. 15), and the City's service must meet WCAG (Act 306/2019). Only UX treated this seriously.
4. **Identity without exclusion.** Scarce signals need one person, one wallet. Suomi.fi e-ID excludes people abroad and new arrivals; verified email is gameable. Add an assisted check at registration, never biometrics.
5. **Fair pilot evaluation.** Giving some jobseekers signals and others none raises equal-treatment questions for a public service. Compare whole events (stepped rollout) and measure outcome parity through voluntary, unlinked surveys, never by collecting ethnicity.
