# Round 2 review — STR (CEO & Business Strategist)

## 1. Overall read

The strongest ideas share one move: stop asking for prose and ask for what AI cannot cheaply manufacture. That means a scarce choice (UX-04, PM-04), a problem-shaped need (TAL-01, UX-02), live proof (UX-07, TAL-06) and a closed loop (PM-08, TAL-08). The best also pay the company back at once: a ten-minute need that reveals real supply (UX-02, TECH-10) is our only credible answer to P1, the brief's first and hardest pain. The convergence is striking: five roles proposed scarce signals, five evidence-first talent cards, and all six a need intake and a pool preview. The core is settled; we win on P1 and on the event format.

Over-engineered: client-side matching (TECH-04), verifiable credentials (TECH-07), and signed passes with solvers and load tests (TECH-09). Invisible to users, expensive in a 20-hour build. Company burden is underweighted almost everywhere: many features add duties (answer every signal, rate, host, close within 72 hours) to the hardest stakeholder to engage.

The Espoo jury will love three moments: ticking "fluent Finnish" and watching the pool collapse; a fair where the CV comes last and proof happens live; and an outcome report the City can send to the next hundred companies.

## 4. My top 10

1. UX-02 — The full P1+P2 loop in one screen: a three-minute need earns instant, private proof of supply, so early and honest needs become self-interest.
2. UX-04 — Sharpest answer to the AI question: scarce requests that pair evidence with a problem line; nothing to ghostwrite, and companies triage evidence, not essays.
3. PM-08 — Closure as a product contract plus a City desk at peak hiring intent; 90-day outcomes become the proof that free is not cheap.
4. TAL-01 — The most pilotable P2 fix: plugs into existing company visits, outputs the assessment kit, and rewards T-21 commitment with slots.
5. UX-07 — Redesigns the fair itself: live problem-solving and structured tents create signal AI cannot fake, pilotable with paper at the next TalentMatch.
6. TECH-10 — Best-engineered Pool Proof: evidence-level bands and "which requirement shrinks your pool" make de-biasing the company's own interest.
7. TAL-08 — Converts the fair's many "no"s into warm peer referrals for people without networks and gives the City a funnel instead of a headcount.
8. RAI-03 — Best talent card: "no quote, no claim" makes polish worthless without detectors; translated cards keep writing fluency out of the signal.
9. TAL-06 — Strongest individual proof with the name last; the natural second-pilot format once the core loop works.
10. TAL-09 — The City's moat: no private platform can attach €5,000 and co-funded training to a match, and the timing warning protects companies.

## 5. Red flags

- **RAI-02** — "Every talent gets at least one conversation" cannot hold at TalentMatch ratios (5–50 employers, 200–1,000 jobseekers; PM estimates ≈180 slots for 300+ people), and forced slots irritate companies. Mitigation: a floor within capacity plus group formats (PM-05 Circles, UX-07 tables).
- **TECH-10, UX-02, PM-02, RAI-05 (and STR-01)** — small-number trap: an opt-in event pool behind k-suppression may show "<5" for niche tech needs and *confirm* "no suitable candidates". Mitigation: register-level aggregates on a confirmed legal basis (RAI-05), per-requirement counts, and a "talk to an adviser" route into the free candidate search.
- **UX-05** — "AI-drafted" versus "own words" chips are a soft AI-detection label that can penalise non-native writers who needed help. Mitigation: label the evidence level, never authorship.
- **TAL-04 (also TECH-07)** — ratings that travel to other employers build a cross-company reputation profile, and City-issued credentials add liability. Mitigation: only talent-controlled, positive facts ("discussed live") leave the rating company; drop verifiable credentials.
- **RAI-07, TAL-05** — counting event conversations toward the job-search obligation can reward box-ticking attendance, and its legal basis is unconfirmed. Mitigation: count only a signal that was spent and answered; keep the firewall either way.
- **TAL-09 (and STR-03)** — subsidy conditions name protected grounds (age, language) before selection, and labelled eligibility backfired in the Dayton voucher experiment. Mitigation: show support at role level, reveal it only after "next step", and let an adviser confirm eligibility.
- **UX-04, UX-09** — AI pairing suggestions edge into Annex III (targeted job ads, candidate evaluation) from 2 December 2027. Mitigation: deterministic overlap rules, logs, human choice and FRIA-ready documentation.
- **TECH-04, TECH-07, TECH-09** — feasibility: a large share of a 20-hour build goes on infrastructure nobody sees. Drop them for the demo; keep the principles.
- **RAI-06, PM-08, UX-10, PM-04** — duties pile onto the least-engaged stakeholder: shared notes, 72-hour closure, answering every signal. Mitigation: two-tap closure, City-run intake, public response rates, a HeRoHub pilot.
- **TAL-06** — perceived unpaid work, adverse impact of work samples, and a "no AI" rule that cannot be enforced online. Mitigation: a 15-minute cap, a practice taster and "AI allowed, as in the job" by default.

## 6. Recommended winning concept

**Name:** TalentMatch Signal — *see the pool, spend a spark, prove it live.*

**Combines:** UX-02, TECH-10, RAI-03, UX-04, UX-07, PM-08, TAL-08. The journey runs Need + Proof (UX-02, TECH-10) → Talent card (RAI-03) → Signal (UX-04) → Live conversation (UX-07) → Closure and routing (PM-08, TAL-08).

**Demo story:** T-21: Business Espoo texts a link to Sanna, founder of a 14-person SaaS firm. Her three-minute memo becomes a Problem Card for HR approval (UX-02). The Supply Radar (TECH-10) shows "20+ registrants with CI/CD evidence". She ticks "fluent Finnish" and it drops to "<5". She switches to "Finnish for customer calls within a year" and the band returns. Approved by T-14, she gets first scheduling access.

Amira, an international backend engineer, tells three work stories by voice. Her Quote-Backed Card (RAI-03) keeps only what she said and visibly refuses "led the cloud migration". She spends one of five Sparks (UX-04), pairing "cut build time from 30 to 6 minutes" with Sanna's "our CI takes 40 minutes". Sanna reviews 12 evidence pairs instead of 140 messages, accepts, and only then sees Amira's name.

Event day: Challenge Table 4, then a table-tent 1:1 asking everyone the same three questions (UX-07). Within 72 hours Sanna taps "next step" (PM-08); the desk warns: "€5,000 Espoo support may apply. Don't sign before the decision." Two others get warm redirects to a peer firm (TAL-08). Her one-page outcome report becomes Business Espoo's pitch to the next hundred companies.

**Cut explicitly:**
- TECH-04 client-side matching.
- TECH-07 credentials.
- TECH-09 signing, solver and load tests; a static offline agenda page is enough.
- RAI-02 stable matching; use mutual accept plus simple slotting.
- A live twin lab; show one TECH-11 result on a slide.
- TAL-06 tasters and the service-needs lanes (TAL-10, STR-08, STR-09), until the pilot.
- Any score, ranking or AI-text detection.

**Biggest risk:** company follow-through. Every talent-side promise (answered Sparks, a table seat, closure within 72 hours) depends on the least-engaged stakeholder. If companies go quiet, we rebuild the black hole with a nicer UI. Mitigation: City staff run the intake on company visits, closure takes two taps, response rates are public, and the pool preview is the reward for committing early.

## 7. Gaps

- **A company effort budget.** Almost every feature adds a duty for the hardest-to-engage stakeholder: intake, answering signals, hosting, rating, closure within 72 hours. Nobody adds it up. Design to at most 30 minutes of company time outside the event day, and let City staff absorb the rest.
- **City staff capacity and the pilot business case.** Desks, Connectors, quality calls, coaches and advisers appear everywhere, but nobody has costed the hours per event. Nobody has tied them to the municipal share of unemployment benefits either, which is what makes a pilot fundable.
- **Integration, not replacement.** Few proposals say how this sits beside the procured event platform (Tavata in 2024) and Job Market Finland. It needs exports, links and a layer the City can buy under the national procurement threshold.
- **One north-star metric with a baseline.** There are dozens of KPIs but no shared one. Proposal: relevant conversations per company-hour, then hires at 90 days, with a baseline from the last TalentMatch before any pilot.
- **Between-event continuity.** Unmet needs and unmatched talent go cold after each event. Only PM-02's standing pool (and STR-07) point to an always-on pipeline that routes leftovers to the next event or to the candidate search.
