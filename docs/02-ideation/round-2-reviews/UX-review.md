# Round 2 review — UX (Head of Design & User Research)

## 1. Overall read

The strongest ideas change what people are asked to produce instead of policing it. Companies state a problem and how they will check it; talents tell real episodes in their own words; interest is spent, not written; capability is shown to a person. All six roles converged independently on the same spine: need intake, pool proof, own-words evidence, scarce requests, structured conversations and closure. That convergence is the product. The job now is to pick one version per cluster and one vocabulary.

Over-engineered: on-device matching with signed catalogs, verifiable credentials, CP-SAT and Gale–Shapley where a mutual-yes rule is enough, savings models, three dashboards and a release gate. Good slides, weak demos. Artefact inflation is a real UX risk: Need, Challenge, Problem, Role Reality and Project Cards; Evidence, Story and Quote cards; Fit Sheets; passports; four evidence ladders. The jury should see one card per side.

Underweighted: people who cannot type fluently or lack a good phone, online participants, and the recruiter's total time budget.

The jury will love "fluent Finnish: 41 → 6", a meeting request made without writing a word, a guard refusing an inflated claim, and nobody leaving in silence.

## 4. My top 10

1. TECH-08 — Sharpest answer to P4+P6: interest is scarce and every request points at a need line with evidence, so no essay exists for AI to write.
2. RAI-03 — Gives talent words without ghostwriting: their own quotes become claims, inflation is refused, fluency is invisible; works in any language.
3. TAL-01 — Fixes P2 at the root: a 12-minute intake with a keep-three game SMEs will finish, producing the conversation kit everything downstream reuses.
4. TECH-06 — Turns event chit-chat into structured, evidence-per-need conversations both sides can see: the signal no AI can fake, at near-zero cost.
5. STR-01 — The 30 seconds that change a sceptical company's mind (P1) while nudging it out of exclusionary requirements; exposes nobody.
6. TAL-08 — Designs the hardest moment, rejection, into routing and warm referrals: dignity for talent, fewer dead ends, and a funnel for the City.
7. TAL-06 — Work before name is the purest "proof, not prose" and the most memorable new event format; fold its essence into the conversation's "show me" minute.
8. TAL-02 — Tackles the main barrier international experts report by turning "fluent Finnish" into task-level, time-phased needs, with support to close gaps.
9. RAI-01 — Identity last, capability first: Helsinki's pilot shows anonymity roughly closes the interview gap, and the identifiability meter makes privacy tangible.
10. PM-08 — Nobody leaves in silence, and "next step: needs permit" opens the City's own toolbox at peak intent; closure data is P1 proof.

## 5. Red flags

No feature breaches the hard constraints: none decides about individuals without a human, needs real personal data for the demo, or gates on AI-text detection.

- **STR-09**: multi-employer contracts, collective agreements and precarity. Drop from the demo; pilot later with Espoon Yrittäjät.
- **TECH-04**: phone-only data excludes shared-device and coach-assisted users and blinds Connectors and Second Look. Keep "rank needs, not people" as a rule over a central opt-in store.
- **TECH-07**: the City vouching for skills means liability and uneven witnessing, and a missing credential reads as failure. Use a "witnessed live" chip instead.
- **STR-03, TAL-09**: eligibility rests on protected grounds (age, mother tongue). Role level only; conversation scripts forbid probing; advisors check after offer intent.
- **STR-05**: linking outcomes to the employment register drifts toward benefit supervision (against RAI-07). Aggregates only, DPO-approved; keep "benefit savings" away from talent.
- **PM-02**: consent from benefit recipients is not freely given (GDPR recital 43), and excerpts at n ≥ 5 risk re-identification. Separate revocable opt-in, k ≥ 10.
- **TAL-04** (also TECH-02, UX-05): company-confirmed levels visible to other employers share evaluations and imply failures. Talent choose what shows; failed attempts leave no trace.
- **TAL-06**: adverse impact and unpaid work. Practice taster, extra time, 15-minute cap, explicit AI rule.
- **STR-08**: spec-work and IP risk, and essays reopen the blank box. Replace with evidence pairing and a live pitch.
- **RAI-01**: banning free text would delete the evidence quotes. Allow quotes; run the identifiability meter on them.
- **RAI-08**: name swaps prove little if names never reach the model. Test language, style, degree and gap twins.
- **PM-04, TAL-07**: exact demand counts herd; seven minutes rushes second-language speakers. Coarse heat; 12–15-minute slots.
- **TECH-03**: brittle ESCO mapping can steer career changers toward lower-paid work. Curated table plus TECH-11 audits.
- **RAI-07**: the "counts as an application" premise is legally unconfirmed. Don't promise it in the pitch.
- **STR-04, PM-04, TAL-05, TECH-08**: scarcity anxiety and misuse by less confident talent (rose study). Refunds, coach help, a multilingual explainer and a walk-in lane.

## 6. Recommended winning concept

**Common Table — proof, not prose.** *Companies bring a problem, talent brings a story, Espoo sets the table.* A signal layer for TalentMatch that runs alongside any event platform.

**Combines (7):** TAL-01 need intake with a language grid · STR-01 Pool Proof · RAI-03 own-words cards · TECH-08 Sparks · TECH-06 Proof Conversation · PM-08 closure and Next-Step desk · TAL-08 redirect.

**Demo story:** T-21: Sanna, an SME founder without HR, opens a link from her Business Espoo account manager. In a 12-minute voice intake she keeps three of ten requirement cards and names the 90-day problem: "our CI pipeline takes 40 minutes" (TAL-01). Ticking "fluent Finnish" drops Pool Proof from 41 to 6, so she writes "English team; Finnish B1 for customer calls by month 6" (STR-01). T-14: Amira, an engineer who moved to Espoo with her spouse, tells two work stories by voice in Arabic. Her card quotes her own words in a labelled translation, and the guard refuses to turn "helped with builds" into "led DevOps" (RAI-03). She spends one of three Sparks pairing Sanna's line with her quote "cut build time from 30 to 6 minutes", no cover letter (TECH-08). Sanna accepts four of 14 evidence pairs; only then do names appear. Event day: nobody logs in, and a printed tent runs a 12-minute Proof Conversation where Sanna taps "demonstrated" per must-have (TECH-06). +72 h: Amira has an interview date, and the City desk books Sanna a support check before she signs (PM-08). A recruiter redirects Jukka, a career changer, to a better-fitting booth: a warm referral, not silence (TAL-08).

**Cut explicitly:** on-device matching and signed catalogs (TECH-04); verifiable credentials (TECH-07); Gale–Shapley and CP-SAT (RAI-02, TECH-09), replaced by a greedy mutual-yes scheduler with a one-before-four floor; the STR-05, STR-07 and PM-09 dashboards, replaced by one organiser screen listing who has zero meetings; STR-02's tiers (one T-14 deadline); STR-08, STR-09 and TAL-10 (a roadmap slide); the Taster Bar build (keep TAL-06's "show me" minute inside the conversation); the release gate and AI register (one TECH-11 parity slide and one "not used" receipt line).

**Single biggest risk:** company effort. We ask already-reluctant companies (P1) for an intake, Spark replies, scripted conversations and 72-hour closure. If that feels like more work than a booth, supply collapses and Sparks have nothing to point at. Mitigation: City account managers run the intake by phone or during visits, Pool Proof and the support desk pay off immediately, and recruiter minutes per event are capped and measured.

## 7. Gaps

1. **Pay and conditions on the card.** Talents spend scarce Sparks blind: nobody shows a salary band, contract type or remote policy, although the EU Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970) gives applicants a right to pay-range information before the interview.
2. **Assisted channels as first-class.** Voice-first flows assume a good phone and speech recognition that handles accents. Easy-to-read Finnish (needed by 11–14% of people in Finland), phone or paper intake and library booths should be designed in, not bolted on.
3. **Online parity.** Tables, tasters, badges and "witnessed live" are physical, yet TalentMatch also runs online and hybrid editions. Online talent need the same evidence rung, with camera-off allowed.
4. **The recruiter's day as one budget.** Intake, inbox, ratings, closure and redirects each look light alone; nobody has added up recruiter minutes per event, and P1 depends on that total being lower than today.
5. **Downstream company AI.** The brief names AI-assisted recruitment, so exported cards will be fed into company ATS ranking. Define a handoff format (quotes, provenance, no score) and participation terms on re-ranking.
