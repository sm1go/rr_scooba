# Round 2 review — TECH (CTO & AI/ML Lead)

## 1. Overall read

The strongest ideas change what is asked for instead of policing how it was written. Four signals that survive generative AI recur in every role: scarce intent (tokens, Sparks), claims anchored in the person's own words, capability witnessed in a structured live format, and needs stated as testable problems. They also share an architecture I endorse: AI works on the company side or as interviewer and translator, deterministic and explainable mechanisms touch personal data, and humans decide. Convergence is striking (five token variants, five talent intakes, four need intakes, five pool previews), so the work now is integration and cutting, not invention.

Over-engineered for ~20 hours: incentive engines, outcome ledgers and demand radars (dashboards need many events of data), Shared Hire, a second project lane, cryptographic credentials and on-device matching (both mine), full fairness release gates, and voice in many languages.

The jury will love moments that explain themselves in ten seconds: "fluent Finnish: 6 people; Finnish B1 for customer calls: 31"; "a bot can write a thousand applications but cannot spend a sixth Spark"; a recruiter's thumb tapping anchored ratings; Wi-Fi off and the day still runs; a provenance chip instead of an AI detector.

## 5. Red flags

No feature triggers the hard-constraint rule (automated decisions about people, real personal data, AI-text detection as a gate); the flags below are design risks.

- **RAI-02** — A one-meeting guarantee is arithmetically impossible at TalentMatch scale (up to 1,000 talents, ~180 slots), and auto-placing people into reserved slots edges toward automated assignment. Mitigate: guarantee a format (Circle, table or Connector intro), not a 1:1; organiser sign-off; published capacity.
- **TAL-06, UX-07** — Work samples show large subgroup gaps (d ≈ .67 in Sackett et al.), group tables favour fluent talkers, and tasks risk becoming unpaid work. Mitigate: optional, practice taster, extra time, write-first rules, synthetic tasks, parity monitoring.
- **STR-03, TAL-09** — Eligibility rests on protected grounds (under 30, foreign-language speaker); naming them on a role card nudges selection by age or language. Mitigate: generic "City support may apply"; criteria only in the advisor step after mutual interest.
- **STR-05** — Outcome tracking by the authority that also supervises benefits, register cross-checks and benefit-savings framing invite function creep and chilled participation. Mitigate: RAI-07 firewall, aggregates only, talent-confirmed outcomes, no savings per person.
- **STR-08** — The first filter is 150 words of prose, the genre LLMs mass-produce, with spec-work and IP exposure. Mitigate: point-don't-write requests or a live pitch first, plus an IP clause; otherwise drop from the MVP.
- **TAL-04** — "Confirmed", set by one company's rating and shown to others, becomes a cross-company evaluation that follows the person (GDPR Art. 22, Annex III). Mitigate: talent-owned, opt-in, positive-only, no grade.
- **UX-09, TAL-08** — AI-suggested pairings of identifiable people likely fall under Annex III(4)(a) from 2 December 2027. Mitigate: unranked, explained, logged, a human decides, FRIA before the pilot.
- **STR-01, PM-02, UX-02, RAI-05** — Small pools invite differencing and re-identification (k = 5 and rounding alone leak; anonymised stories can identify), and using employment-service records needs a confirmed legal basis. Mitigate: k ≥ 10, stable noise, query budget, fixed query shapes, RAI-01's meter on excerpts.
- **UX-01, RAI-03, PM-03, TAL-04** — Speech recognition and LLM extraction vary by language and accent (P10), and talent text can carry prompt injections. Mitigate: text path, editable transcripts, quote check outside the LLM, per-language parity tests before release.
- **RAI-06** — Dictated impressions shared by default may carry off-criteria or discriminatory remarks. Mitigate: ticks first, vetted phrases, free text optional.
- **STR-09** — Multi-employer contracts, collective agreements and competition law for a rare case. Drop from the MVP.

## 6. Recommended winning concept

**Name:** TalentMatch Signal — proof, not prose (PM and STR chose the name independently; it slots into Espoo's existing TalentMatch series).

**Combines:** PM-01, STR-01, RAI-03, UX-04, PM-06, UX-05, PM-07, as merged in section 2 (PM-01 absorbs RAI-04's lint rules, UX-04 PM-04's answer-every-signal rule, PM-06 PM-08's one-tap closure). My TECH equivalents merge in without adding screens.

**Demo story (3 minutes):** T-21: the operations manager of Kuura Logistics (synthetic) dictates her need for three minutes. The card compiles with ESCO chips, an evidence question ("how would you check this in 15 minutes?") and a flag on "fluent Finnish" (PM-01). Pool Proof answers her doubt: 6 people; reworded to "Finnish B1 for customer calls from month 6", 31 (STR-01). She commits. Amina, a planner with a caregiving gap, tells two work stories by voice in English; each claim appears beside her own quote, and the guard refuses an inflated one (RAI-03). Chips read "own words" (UX-05), and her ChatGPT-polished twin earns no higher rung: polish buys nothing, evidence climbs. She spends one of five Sparks, pairing "re-routed 40 deliveries after a depot closed" with Kuura's line "routes break when a truck fails" (UX-04). The recruiter answers 14 evidence pairs instead of 140 messages, names hidden until the recruiter accepts. Event day: we switch off the Wi-Fi and the pass still works (PM-07). The 15-minute meeting runs on three card-derived questions, thumb-tapped anchors and a "show me" minute (PM-06); Amina's claim turns "witnessed live", and one tap sends her next step within 72 hours. No CV, no score, no AI judgement.

**Cut explicitly:** person-level scores, rankings and fit percentages; AI-text detection; recording and voice or emotion analysis. For the build: incentives (STR-03, TAL-09) and dashboards (STR-05, STR-07, PM-09) become one slide; Shared Hire, Project Lane and Challenge Market wait for the pilot; Circles, remote seats and badges are narrated, not built; my credentials and on-device matching (TECH-07, TECH-04) become roadmap; the Twin Lab shrinks to one plain-vs-polished check; voice in two languages only.

**Single biggest risk:** scope versus ~20 hours. The two LLM steps (compiling the need, extracting quoted claims) are fragile, and if they stumble on stage everything looks fake. Build the deterministic spine first (cards, counts, Sparks, conversation screen, cached pass) on synthetic data, then add schema-constrained LLM calls over a cached ESCO subset, each with a recorded fallback. Sparks pair card lines chosen by people, so ESCO errors only touch counts and suggestions.

## 7. Gaps

1. **Adversarial input to LLM steps.** 41% of US jobseekers admit prompt-injection tricks (Greenhouse 2025), and every intake feeds talent text to a model. Needed: checks outside the model, schema-only output, no tool access, and injection cases in the twin tests.
2. **One person, one wallet.** Scarcity beats bots only if accounts are costly. Nobody specified identity: Suomi.fi excludes talent abroad and e-mail is trivial to multiply. Bind passes to registration and check-in; staff review duplicates, never biometrics.
3. **Market arithmetic.** Up to 1,000 talents, 5–50 companies, ~180 slots: nobody simulated signal budgets, invite caps and Circles against capacity. A synthetic-market simulator should set budgets per event and tell talent honestly what to expect.
4. **Integration surface.** Espoo procures Tavata-type platforms, and Job Market Finland already holds mandatory ESCO-coded profiles. Nobody specified profile import, agenda export (ICS) or a card API; without them every participant enters data twice.
5. **Retention versus contestability.** 30-day deletion (UX-10, PM-06) may destroy records a talent needs to contest discrimination, and exports to company ATSs change controller roles. Set retention and controller terms with the DPO.
