# Round 2 review — PM (Head of Product)

## 1. Overall read

Fifty-eight ideas collapse into about fifteen features: the roles converged strongly. The best share one move. They change what is asked for instead of policing how it was written: a concrete problem instead of a job ad, quote-backed evidence instead of a CV, a scarce signal instead of a message, a structured live conversation instead of small talk, an answer instead of silence. They need little or no AI on the people side, so J3 comes cheaply. Over-engineered: on-device matching with signed catalogues (TECH-04), verifiable credentials (TECH-07), stable-matching rankings (RAI-02) and twin labs as product screens. With 5–50 employers, talent can read every card. Matching is not the bottleneck; recruiter attention and early company commitment are. Several promises ignore capacity: "every talent gets a meeting" cannot hold with 300+ professionals and about 180 slots. The jury will love three moments: a pool counter jumping when "fluent Finnish" becomes "Finnish B1 by month 6", an AI that refuses to inflate a claim, and a recruiter answering 14 evidence pairs instead of 140 messages. Within clusters my J1 scores barely differ, so read J2, J3 and the comments for variant quality.

## 5. Red flags

- **RAI-02, UX-09** — "Every talent gets at least one conversation" fails the slot math: 300+ professionals against ~180 one-to-one slots (20 tables × 9), or up to 1,000 against 5–50 employers. Mitigation: guarantee meetings only for mutual yeses; promise everyone else a Circle seat, a Connector intro or card feedback.
- **UX-05** — "Own words" versus "AI-drafted" chips will correlate with language background and penalise people who need AI to write in a second language (P10, C5). Mitigation: rungs describe evidence type only; translations labelled neutrally.
- **UX-10 (also PM-08, TAL-08)** — Deleting outcomes and reason codes after 30 days can collide with the one-year limit for recruitment-discrimination claims (Non-Discrimination Act, shared burden of proof). Mitigation: contacts fade at 30 days; a minimal outcome-and-reason record is kept about 12 months.
- **TAL-04** — "Confirmed" from one company's rubric, shown to other recruiters, is a cross-company person score that others draw on (SCHUFA logic, C1). Mitigation: holder-controlled, positive-only, never aggregated, as in TECH-07.
- **STR-05** — Cross-checking outcomes against the employment-services register breaks the voluntariness firewall (RAI-07) and purpose limitation (C3). Mitigation: two-sided self-reports and aggregates only; any register link statistical and DPO-approved.
- **RAI-01** — Switching off free text at rung 0 hides the quote evidence the story cluster relies on, and a k=5 meter on multi-skill cards will flag nearly everyone. Mitigation: show quotes; scan only named entities such as employers and places.
- **TAL-06** — Work samples show large subgroup differences (d≈.67, US data), invite unpaid-work criticism and can flood reviewers (P10, P4). Mitigation: signal-gated, capped, a practice taster, extra time on request.
- **UX-04, UX-09, RAI-06** — AI "stories that fit", AI pairing suggestions and AI mapping of a rep's impressions recommend or evaluate people: Annex III territory, and profiling is never exempt under Art. 6(3) (C4). Mitigation: deterministic overlap, rep ticks and typed lines only.
- **STR-08** — 150-word written approaches make AI-writable prose the screening gate again (P6). Mitigation: replace with evidence pairs, or drop.
- **TECH-04, TECH-07, STR-09** — Local-first matching adds device-loss failure modes without removing City controllership; City-issued credentials imply certification liability; shared hires need multi-employer labour law. Drop from the 48h build.
- **RAI-08** — Name swaps on components that never see names risk fairness theatre in front of the jury. Mitigation: headline TECH-11's style and language parity.
- No feature relies on automated decisions without human involvement, real personal data or AI-text detection, so none received the J3 ≤ 2 cap.

## 6. Recommended winning concept

**Name:** TalentMatch Signal — proof, not prose.

**Combines (6 raw IDs):** TAL-01, STR-01, RAI-03, UX-04, TECH-06, TAL-08. Borrowed details, no separate screens: TAL-02/UX-03's language grid, TAL-03's permit stance and RAI-04's lint inside the card; TAL-05's three-signal budget and answer deadline and TECH-08's refunds inside Sparks.

**Demo story:** T-21: a Business Espoo adviser sends Kuura Logistics, a synthetic SME, one link. The operations manager builds a Role Reality Card in 12 minutes (TAL-01): three 90-day outcomes, three must-haves, three conversation questions. Pool Proof (STR-01) shows 41 matching people; ticking "fluent Finnish" drops it to 6, so she writes "English to start, Finnish B1 by month 6" and commits. Amina, a supply-chain planner returning after a caregiving gap, tells three work stories in English (RAI-03); the guard refuses "advanced analytics" because she never said it. In signal week she spends one of three Sparks (UX-04), pairing her quote "cut re-planning from two hours to twenty minutes" with Kuura's line "routes collapse when a truck breaks down". No cover letter. The recruiter answers 14 evidence pairs instead of 140 messages and sees Amina's name only after accepting. On the day, an 8-minute Proof Conversation (TECH-06) runs on Kuura's own questions plus a two-minute "show me". Within 48 hours Amina has a next step, and Mikko, a chef, is redirected with a warm-referral badge to a food-tech booth (TAL-08). The organiser sees needs in by T-14, signals answered and loops closed.

**Cut explicitly:** TECH-04 on-device matching and TECH-07 credentials; RAI-02 ranking (fill mutual yeses greedily); every people-side AI suggestion (UX-04, UX-09, RAI-06 memo mapping); voice input and languages beyond English and Finnish (one recorded clip); the offline pass (a static agenda page plus one architecture slide); the twin lab as a screen (one TECH-11 parity chart); Challenge Tables, Tasters and badges (one pilot-format slide); incentive engines (one "may apply, don't sign yet" banner); Shared Hire, Challenge Market, Project Lane, the Pledge and the savings model.

**Single biggest risk:** company commitment. The chain starts with a 12-minute card at T-21 and ends with answering every Spark by T-4; if SMEs skip either, everything downstream starves. Mitigation: City staff run the intake during existing company visits, Pool Proof is the instant payoff, and the answer duty is a participation term.

## 7. Gaps

1. **Capacity math and an honest promise.** With 200–1,000 talents and 5–50 employers, most talents get no 1:1. Design and pitch what everyone else receives (Circle seat, Connector intro, card feedback), online participants included.
2. **The two-sided calendar.** Pool Proof at T-21 needs talent cards before companies commit; signals at T-10 need company cards. Nobody drew the T-30 to T+90 timeline with both sides' deadlines, nudges and a standing consented pool between events.
3. **A company time budget.** Intake, taster, rubric, signals, closure and pledge add up to hours for an SME without HR. Cap it (e.g. 45 minutes before the event, 10 after), measure it and sell it.
4. **The employer's own AI after export.** We constrain the City's AI, but companies can pipe exported cards into AI screeners: the other half of the synthetic loop (P6). Participation terms plus minimal, structured exports.
5. **One north-star metric with a baseline.** Metrics are scattered. Pick one (next steps per recruiter-hour, or hires and projects at 90 days per event), baseline it on the last TalentMatch and A/B-test one track.
