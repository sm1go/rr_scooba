## Questions

Each question tests a hypothesis (pain points P1–P10, constraints C1–C6 from the challenge brief).

### Must ask

1. **(P2)** When do companies usually send you their talent needs, and in what form — job ad, e-mail,
   call? Roughly what share arrives in the last week before the event? Who fills it in: HR or the
   hiring manager?
2. **(P2, UX)** If a hiring manager could record a 3-minute voice note instead of writing a description,
   would companies do it — and earlier than today?
3. **(P1)** What would convince a sceptical company that your pool contains suitable candidates? Would an
   anonymous count ("41 registered people have shown this skill") help, or would it feel risky?
4. **(P3, P4)** What do you collect at registration today, and which fields actually help you match?
   Typical numbers: registrations per event, meeting requests a company receives vs. accepts, no-shows.
5. **(P6)** Do you see AI-written profiles and messages? How do recruiters react? Would a cap on meeting
   requests per person (3–5 "priority requests") feel fair to jobseekers?
6. **(P7, P8)** Which event platform do you use now, and what exactly breaks at the start of an event —
   login, schedules, video?
7. **(C1–C6)** Your red lines for a pilot: what may the City store and for how long, DPO / DPIA
   requirements, languages (Finnish / Swedish / English), accessibility requirements?

### If time allows

8. **(P4, P6)** Would recruiters accept a short structured format for 1:1s — the same three questions
   for every candidate, 10–12 minutes, a guaranteed next step?
9. **(C1)** Who would be the human in the loop on the event day (e.g. a matchmaker desk)? How many staff
   do you have per event?
10. **(P1, P10)** Do you know what happens after events (interviews, hires)? What would you need to prove
    the value of events to companies and to City leadership?
11. **(P9)** For international talent: which questions come up most — permits, language, relocation,
    family, recognition of qualifications?
